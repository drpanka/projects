Channels, not postings

Four of these don't list a job today but are how corridor openings actually surface. Set them up once and the next cycle comes to you.

CHS Mailbag

MDH's weekly digest of every Minnesota local and tribal health department opening, delivered to your inbox. The single highest-yield subscription for county public health work in this corridor.

Minnesota Merit System

The shared civil-service register for county human services. Continuous “future vacancies” exams place you on a list that Houston, Rice, Wabasha, and Olmsted counties hire from directly.

Olmsted County job alerts

Epidemiologist 1/2 and Public Health Strategist recur here and nowhere else in the corridor. Alerts are worth setting even while nothing is posted.

wisc.jobs

Carries Wisconsin county postings alongside state ones — Sauk and Jefferson counties both appear there now. It is the one board to watch for La Crosse, Vernon, and Crawford.

Shakopee Mdewakanton Sioux Community

Tribal government in Prior Lake and Shakopee, hiring health and behavioral health roles in the southwest metro. Sovereign employer, separate board, frequently overlooked.

Tomah VA & Minneapolis VA

Federal health roles at both ends of the corridor. Currently clinical rather than analytic, but VA data and informatics reqs post through the same USAJOBS announcements.

What I'd do this week

  1. Apply to Planner Principal State (95688) first. The doctorate substitution takes the three-year requirement down to one, and it's the only one of the four where no clause quietly requires prior government employment. Five days left.

  2. Then 96838, today if possible. It closes in two days and it is the one that names dashboards and Power BI outright — the closest match to where you're taking your analytics work.

  3. Write the resume to the State of Minnesota's rules, not to a normal resume standard. Every one of these postings repeats the same warning: list beginning and ending month and year for each role, and describe experience in the exact areas listed. State HR screens minimum qualifications mechanically before a hiring manager ever reads it. A resume that would impress a private employer routinely fails this filter.

  4. Claim the doctorate substitution explicitly. Don't assume the screener infers it. Name the degree, name the clause, and state the arithmetic — two years of the requirement, satisfied by the doctorate.

  5. Subscribe to the CHS Mailbag and set Olmsted alerts. Both are free and both cover the gap that this week's sweep exposed: the corridor hires in bursts, and you want to hear about the next one before it's on a job board.

The honest gap

Most epidemiologist and health-analyst roles in this corridor gate on an MPH or demonstrated applied analytics in a public health setting — Olmsted's own Epidemiologist 1 posting asks for an MPH, or a bachelor's plus a year of applied epidemiology in a community setting. A clinical doctorate plus a data science certificate is a genuinely strong combination, but it reads to a government screener as neither credential. Two things close that gap faster than a second degree: the doctorate-for-experience substitutions the State of Minnesota writes into nearly every posting, and any position inside a governmental public health agency, which converts the recurring “experience working for state, local, or tribal public health” clause from a barrier into a checked box. That is the real argument for the Olmsted community health worker role despite the pay.

One assumption to correct if I have it wrong: I inferred your background — naturopathic doctorate, clinical practice in Hopkins, and self-directed data science work — from this repository and your email domain rather than from a resume. If you have an MPH, GIS or ArcPy experience, or prior public sector employment, the ranking above changes materially, and the geospatial Research Scientist 3 role moves to the top.
