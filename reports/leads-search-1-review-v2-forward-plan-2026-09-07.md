## 2. Negatives: what to add now, what to watch

Your concern is right: "erectile", "primary care" and specialist names describe things you actually treat. The rule that resolves it is: a negative is safe when the query is looking for something you cannot deliver in Minnesota (a prescription, a conventional specialty credential, IVF), and it belongs on a watch list when the query names a condition you treat but the searcher probably wants the conventional route. Watch-list negatives are removed the day you decide to market that service line, and the Acuity intake answers will tell you when that is worth doing.

- Intro calls are the front door: 31 of 58 self-bookings. Median 2 days between booking and the call, so open intro-call slots in the next week are the constraint that matters, not first-appointment slots.
- Dr. Haley is the demand engine and the bottleneck: 26 of 36 intro calls, 7 of 8 first appointments booked in August. Dr. Jacob: 10 intro calls, 1 August first appointment. Whatever service lines route to Jacob (men's health, functional medicine, part of pediatrics) have capacity; Haley's do not.
